On this page

    Aim

    Finish well: reflect, talk, check

    The last stage has three parts: the viva voce (your final reflection session), your reflective statement (Criterion E, 4 marks), and a final check of everything you submit.

    By the end of this stage you can:

    • prepare for the viva so it is a conversation, not a test
    • write a reflective statement that evaluates your growth, with specific examples
    • check your essay and form against every requirement before you submit
    The final session

    The viva voce

    The viva voce is a short concluding interview with your supervisor after you have handed in your final essay. It is a chance to talk about what you learned, and it also helps your supervisor confirm the work is your own. Your essay is not changed after the viva.

    • Reread your essay and your researcher's reflection space the day before.
    • Be ready to explain any study in your essay in your own words, without notes.
    • Think of a real problem you hit and how you solved it, and a moment your thinking changed.
    • Practise with the mock viva.
    Criterion E

    The reflective statement

    500words at most; anything beyond is not read
    1statement, written after the viva
    RPFit goes on the Reflection and progress form
    4marks for Criterion E

    Criterion E asks how the EE learning experience affected you as a learner. It is not a diary of meetings. The strongest statements are evaluative throughout and use specific examples.

    Describes (earns little)Evaluates (earns more)
    "I met my supervisor three times and changed my question.""Narrowing my question after the census taught me that a good question is one the evidence can answer, not the one I find most exciting."
    "I improved my time management.""Logging my hours showed me I had spent twice my plan on reading; I now set a stopping rule before I start researching anything."
    "I learned a lot about research.""Finding that the famous study had not replicated changed how I read headlines: I now look for who has tried to repeat a result."

    Ideas to draw on: a decision you made and why; a moment your thinking changed; a skill you developed and where you will use it next; what the viva conversation made you realise.

    Yours alone

    An AI-written reflection is not acceptable, and would be fabrication. Your researcher's reflection space is the raw material: use your own notes from the journey.

    Exercise 1 · 25 minutes

    Mark four reflections

    Four short reflective statements, written for practice by students who wrote on the example question (real ones can be up to 500 words). Put them in order from weakest to strongest and give each a mark from 1 to 4, using the "describes or evaluates" table in the Learn tab. Give one reason for each mark.

    Reflection W

    My first reflection session was in March, when my supervisor and I talked about my topic. I chose note-taking because I use a laptop. In my second session in September we discussed my draft and she said my question was good. I found eight studies and read them, and I used APA referencing. I submitted my essay in November. In the viva we talked about my essay and my sources. The EE was hard work, but I finished it on time and I am happy with my final essay.

    Reflection X

    The moment that changed my essay was finding Urry et al. (2021). I had searched only for studies that supported handwriting, because I expected to prove it, and my first plan was one-sided. Seeing a careful replication find no difference made me realise I had been doing exactly what my essay criticised: looking for evidence that confirmed what I already believed. I rewrote my main claim to say the effect depends on reviewing notes, and I added a challenge search to every search I ran afterwards. In the viva, explaining why I kept a weak early study made me see that evaluating evidence means deciding how much it can carry, not whether it is good or bad. I now use the same habit in my Paper 2 answers and when I read health news: I ask who has tried to repeat a result before I believe it. Next time, I would run a study census before falling in love with a question.

    Reflection Y

    I learned a lot about research during the EE. At first it was difficult to find sources, but it got easier as I went on. I also improved my time management, because there were many deadlines. My supervisor helped me a lot and gave me useful feedback on my draft. I think my evaluation got better over time. Overall, the EE was a valuable experience and I developed many skills that will help me at university.

    Reflection Z

    At first I read only abstracts, and my notes were full of conclusions I could not evaluate. When my supervisor asked how one study measured learning, I could not answer, and that was a turning point: I went back and read the method sections of all my core studies, which is where most of my evaluation came from. I realised the abstract of one study did not mention that its groups were not randomly allocated, and that changed how much weight I gave it. I also improved my time management and my referencing, and I enjoyed the topic.

    Model answer

    Try the exercise first, then compare.

    ReflectionOur markWhy
    W1A record of meetings and events. It describes what happened but never evaluates what the student learned or how they changed.
    X4Consistently evaluative, with specific examples of changed thinking (noticing confirmation bias in their own searching, changing the main claim after the replications) and a concrete transfer of skills beyond the EE.
    Y2Some reflection, but general and unsupported: "I learned a lot about research" and "time management" with no specific example of how thinking changed.
    Z3Evaluative and specific about one turning point (reading the method section) and its effect, but the rest stays general and there is little about how the skills transfer.

    These marks use our own descriptions of what earns the marks, not the official markband. The step from 3 to 4 is about consistency and transfer: every part evaluates, and the student shows where the learning goes next.

    Exercise 3 · 20 minutes

    Plan your reflective statement

    From your researcher's reflection space, pick three specific moments: a decision you made, a time your thinking changed, and a skill you will use again. For each, note what happened, why it mattered and what you will do differently. Keep it as a plan; write the statement itself after the viva.

    Check yourself

    Four quick questions

    When is the viva voce, and can you change your essay afterwards?

    After you hand in the final essay. The essay is not changed after the viva.

    Is a record of your three meetings a good reflective statement?

    No. Describing events earns little. Evaluate how the experience changed you, with specific examples.

    What happens to words beyond 500 in the reflective statement?

    They are not read or assessed.

    Can an AI tool help write your reflection?

    No. The reflection must be your own account of your learning; an AI-written one would be fabrication.
