Use AI responsibly

Use AI to help you learn, never to pretend you did something you did not, and be open about every use.

On this page

    Aim

    Two principles

    The IB's guidance on AI in coursework comes down to two questions. Did you use AI to help you learn? That is fine. Did you use AI to pretend you did something you did not? That is not.

    By the end of this stage you can:

    • sort common uses of AI into acceptable, ask first, and not acceptable
    • acknowledge any AI use in the way the IB expects
    • check every reference yourself, because AI tools invent convincing ones
    • reference in APA 7, and use an AI prompt that checks your formatting without touching your writing
    Your school decides too

    The IB says there are no hard rules and leaves room for teachers' judgement. Your school's AI policy and your supervisor can be stricter than this page. When in doubt, ask before you use a tool, not after.

    The rules

    Five rules for your EE

    1. Check every reference yourself. AI tools invent references that look real, or attach a real DOI to a paper that does not exist. Open each source before it goes in your essay.
    2. Mark and reference AI text. If you copy or paraphrase anything an AI tool produced, put it in quotation marks and reference the tool, with the prompt you gave it and the date, in your school's referencing style.
    3. Keep your trail. Keep your drafts, notes and AI chat history. Your supervisor may ask to see them or to talk with you about your essay.
    4. Write an AI use statement. Add a short statement at the end of your work describing how you used AI, even for uses that need no reference.
    5. The reflection is yours alone. No AI tool can tell the story of your own learning.
    Why not just ask AI to "improve the structure"?

    Some EE guides suggest it. We do not, because improving the structure of an argument is improving the argument, and the argument must be yours. The IB treats a rewrite that improves the coherence of your argument as a wholesale rewrite.

    Be open

    How to acknowledge AI

    The IB's focus is transparency, not perfect formatting: a reader must be able to see what is yours and what is not. There are three places AI use can appear.

    WhereWhenPattern
    In the textYou quote or paraphrase AI outputWhen prompted with "[your prompt]" on [date], [Tool] suggested "[words you quote]" ([Company], [year]).
    In the reference listWhenever you cite it in the text[Company]. ([Year]). [Tool name] ([version]) [Large language model]. [URL]
    AI use statementAlways, after the end of your essay"I used [tool] on [dates] to [purpose]. I checked everything it suggested against the original sources, and all analysis and wording in this essay are my own."

    Check before you cite

    Check a reference in two minutes

    1. Open the DOI. Type https://doi.org/ followed by the DOI. Does it open, and is it the same paper?
    2. Search the exact title in quotation marks in Google Scholar. A real paper almost always appears.
    3. Match every detail: authors and their order, year, journal, volume, issue and pages.
    4. Read it. Make sure the paper says what you claim it says. Be ready to explain any source at your viva.

    One style

    Why APA 7

    The IB does not require a particular style. It requires that you reference consistently, in a way that lets a reader find every source. Psychology uses APA (7th edition), so that is the style for your EE.

    APA has two parts that must match: a short in-text citation every time you use a source, and a full entry in the reference list at the end. Every citation needs an entry, and every entry needs at least one citation.

    In the text

    In-text citations

    CaseIn bracketsIn the sentence
    One author(Kiewra, 1989)Kiewra (1989) argued...
    Two authors(Mueller & Oppenheimer, 2014)Mueller and Oppenheimer (2014) found...
    Three or more(Morehead et al., 2019)Morehead et al. (2019) found...
    Group authorFirst time: (World Health Organization [WHO], 2017). Then: (WHO, 2017)The World Health Organization (WHO, 2017) reported...
    Direct quotation(Author, year, p. 12)Author (year) wrote that "..." (p. 12).
    Two or more works(Morehead et al., 2019; Urry et al., 2021)Alphabetical order, separated by semicolons
    A source you only read about(Original author, year, as cited in Author you read, year)Only list the source you actually read. Better: find and read the original.

    Use "&" inside brackets and "and" in the sentence. "Et al." is used from the first citation when there are three or more authors.

    At the end

    The reference list

    • Title it References. Put entries in alphabetical order by the first author's surname.
    • Use a hanging indent: the first line of each entry starts at the margin and the rest are indented.
    • Write surnames, then initials. Keep the authors in the order the article gives. List up to 20 authors; for 21 or more, list the first 19, then ". . .", then the last author.
    • Article and book titles use sentence case: capitals only for the first word, the first word after a colon, and names.
    • Journal names use capitals for main words. The journal name and the volume number are in italics; the issue number in brackets is not.
    • Write the DOI as a link: https://doi.org/.... No full stop after a DOI or URL.

    Templates

    TypePattern
    Journal articleAuthor, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title of the article: Subtitle in sentence case. Journal Name, volume(issue), first page–last page. https://doi.org/xxxx
    BookAuthor, A. A. (Year). Title of the book: Subtitle in sentence case (2nd ed.). Publisher.
    Chapter in an edited bookAuthor, A. A. (Year). Title of the chapter. In E. E. Editor (Ed.), Title of the book (pp. xx–xx). Publisher.
    Report by an organisationOrganisation Name. (Year). Title of the report. URL
    Web pageAuthor, A. A., or Organisation. (Year, Month Day). Title of the page. Site Name. URL

    No date? Write (n.d.). No author? Use the organisation. If you cannot tell who wrote a page or when, ask whether it belongs in your essay at all.

    Avoid these

    Common mistakes

    In the text

    • Listing all three authors instead of "et al."
    • "and" inside brackets instead of "&"
    • A quotation with no page number
    • A citation with no matching reference

    In the reference list

    • Article titles in Title Case
    • "et al." in the reference list
    • Authors in a different order from the article
    • "doi:" instead of a https://doi.org/ link

    Check yourself

    Five quick questions

    An AI tool gives you a reference with a working DOI. Is that enough to cite it?

    No. A common AI mistake is attaching a real DOI to an invented paper. Check that the DOI opens the same paper, with the same authors, year and title, and read the paper before you cite it.

    You used AI only to suggest search terms. Do you need to mention it?

    Yes, in your AI use statement. It needs no in-text citation because no AI text appears in your essay, but hiding any AI use is not acceptable.

    Why is asking AI to rewrite your essay "so it flows better" not acceptable?

    A rewrite changes who wrote the essay. If it improves the coherence of your argument, the IB treats it as a wholesale rewrite.

    What three things does the IB expect when you quote AI output?

    Quotation marks, a reference to the tool in your school's style, and the prompt you gave it with the date.

    How do you cite a paper with three authors in APA 7?

    First author's surname, "et al.", year, from the very first citation: (Morehead et al., 2019). In the reference list, all three authors are listed in full.
