    Aim

    Know the task before you start

    The extended essay is an independent piece of research of up to 4,000 words. In psychology it is a review of published research: you analyse peer-reviewed studies on one focused question, build an argument, weigh the evidence against it, and reach a reasoned judgement.

    By the end of this stage you can:

    • say what a psychology EE is, and what it is not allowed to be
    • spot topics that will not work before you spend hours on them
    • explain the five criteria in your own words
    • plan your 40 hours and your three reflection sessions
    Our example questionTo what extent does taking notes by hand rather than on a laptop improve students' learning from lectures?

    The task

    What a psychology EE is

    It is

    • A review of peer-reviewed research on one focused question
    • About behaviour or cognitive processes, usually through a biological, cognitive or sociocultural lens
    • An argument: a clear line from question to judgement, with counter-arguments weighed
    • Aware of complexity and of what research still cannot tell us

    It is not

    • Your own experiment, survey, interview or observation (not allowed)
    • A description of a topic, or a summary of a textbook
    • An explanation of how a biological mechanism works, without the behaviour
    • A personal opinion piece
    4,000words at most; examiners stop reading after that
    About 40hours of your own time
    3reflection sessions: first, interim and the viva voce
    500words at most for your reflective statement, written after the viva
    Avoid these

    Topics that will not work

    The IB guidance names several kinds of topic that are not appropriate for a psychology EE. Spot them early.

    Kind of topicExampleWhy it fails
    Your own dataA survey of students at your schoolPrimary data collection is not allowed
    Literature interpretationThe psychology of a character in a novelNot research on real behaviour
    Psychoanalysing public figuresWas a famous leader a narcissist?No research exists on that person to review
    Speculating about a trendHow will a new app change teenagers' thinking?Published research cannot answer it yet
    HistoriesHow treatment of mental illness changed over a centuryA historical account, not a review of research
    Pure biologyHow does a neurotransmitter work?The focus must be how a process affects behaviour
    A theory applied to something newSchema theory and a viral trendOnly works if published studies test the theory in that situation
    Talk to your supervisor early

    Some topics need careful handling and early approval from your supervisor, for example romantic relationships, religion, abuse, or any topic that is close to home for you. Your well-being matters more than your topic. If a topic upsets you, choose another one, and talk to the school counsellor if you need to.

    The journey

    How the process runs

    1. First reflection session. Discuss your topic and early question with your supervisor. Bring your first searches.
    2. Research and plan. Find and judge sources, read and take notes, and build your argument.
    3. Interim reflection session. Discuss your progress and your argument so far.
    4. One full draft. Your supervisor reads and comments on one complete draft. They will not edit it for you, so make it a genuine, complete draft.
    5. Final essay. Revise, check and submit the final version.
    6. Viva voce. A short final interview with your supervisor about your work and what you learned. The essay is not changed afterwards.
    7. Reflective statement. Write your statement of up to 500 words after the viva, on the Reflection and progress form.
    Keep a researcher's reflection space

    A notebook, document or folder where you record searches, ideas, problems and changes of mind as they happen. It makes the reflection sessions and your final statement far easier, and it is where every numbered task on these pages goes.

    Your own work

    Academic integrity

    • Every idea, finding and quotation from a source is cited, every time.
    • Keep your notes, drafts and search log. They show how your essay grew.
    • Any AI use follows the rules on Stage 8 and goes in your AI use statement.
    • Your supervisor confirms the essay is your own work. The viva is part of that, so be ready to explain anything in your essay.
    For supervisors

    Notes for supervisors

    Suggested use
    • Use Exercise 1 as the opening activity of the EE launch lesson; it surfaces unsuitable topics before students invest in them.
    • Ask students to bring their completed hours plan to the first reflection session.
    • Flag sensitive topics early and agree a plan, in line with school policy.
    Exercise 1 · 15 minutes

    Is this an EE?

    Sort each title into suitable, needs fixing or not allowed, with one reason. For any that need fixing, suggest how.

    1. To what extent does sleep deprivation affect working memory in adolescents?
    2. A survey of students' study habits and their grades at our school
    3. How does dopamine work in the brain?
    4. What does Hamlet's indecision reveal about his psychology?
    5. Was a famous historical leader a narcissist?
    6. The history of how mental illness was treated in the twentieth century
    7. Is social media bad for teenagers?
    8. To what extent is mindfulness training in schools effective in reducing anxiety in adolescents?
    9. Nature or nurture: what causes intelligence?
    10. How will a new video app change the way teenagers think over the next decade?

    Exercise 2 · 15 minutes

    Your time map

    Plan your 40 hours. Use the hours tracker or a table in your researcher's reflection space.

    1. Split the 40 hours across the stages. Reading and writing usually take the most time.
    2. Write in your school's dates for the first reflection session, the interim session, the draft deadline, the final deadline and the viva.
    3. Mark two weeks that will be busy with other deadlines and plan around them.

    A starting split

    Choosing and testing a question 4 hours, finding sources 6, reading and notes 10, building the argument 4, writing the draft 10, revising after feedback 4, final checks and reflection 2. Adjust it to suit you.

    Check yourself

    Five quick questions

    Can you run a short survey to support your psychology EE?

    No. The psychology EE is a review of published research; your own experiments, interviews and surveys are not allowed.

    Your topic is how a neurotransmitter works. What is the problem?

    It is about a biological mechanism, not behaviour. Refocus on how the process affects a behaviour or a cognitive process, and on a question the research debates.

    Which criterion carries the most marks, and what does that mean for your writing?

    Discussion and evaluation (D, 8 marks). Evaluation should run through the whole essay, not sit in one section at the end.

    When do you write your reflective statement, and how long can it be?

    After the viva voce, at the end of the process. Up to 500 words, on the Reflection and progress form.

    Your supervisor reads your draft. Will they correct it for you?

    No. They read and comment on one complete draft, but they do not edit it. Make that draft genuine and complete.
